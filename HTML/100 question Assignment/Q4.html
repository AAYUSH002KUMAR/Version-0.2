What is DNS and when does it come into play?

DNS (Domain Name System) is a system that translates human-readable domain names (like www.example.com) into IP addresses (like 192.168.1.1) that computers use to identify each other on the internet.

DNS comes into play whenever you type a URL into your browser or when your computer needs to communicate with another device on the internet. It acts as a phonebook for the internet, allowing you to access websites using easy-to-remember domain names instead of complex IP addresses.
